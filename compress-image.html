    How to use Compress Image to KB

    1. Choose your photo (JPG, PNG or WebP). On a phone you can pick it from your gallery.
    2. Pick the size your form asks for, such as Under 20 KB, Under 50 KB or Under 100 KB. If your size is not in the list, choose Custom size and type the number of KB.
    3. Press Compress Image, check the new size and the preview, then download your photo.

    When to use Compress Image to KB

    Many online forms for jobs, exams, admissions and government services ask for a passport-size photo or a signature with a strict size limit. Common limits are between 10 KB and 100 KB, but a photo from a modern phone is usually 2 to 5 MB. If the image is too big, the website refuses it.

    This tool makes the photo smaller until it fits under the limit you choose. It first lowers the JPG quality a little, and only makes the picture smaller in width and height if that is still not enough. This keeps your photo as clear as possible.

    Tips for the best result

    • Crop the photo to just your face or signature before compressing. A smaller picture area keeps more detail at the same file size.
    • Take the photo in good daylight against a plain light background. Clean photos compress better than dark or noisy ones.
    • Choose a target slightly below the limit. The tool already keeps the file a little under the size you pick, so it is accepted whether the website counts 1 KB as 1,000 or 1,024 bytes.
    • Need the photo inside a PDF? Use JPG to PDF, then Compress PDF with a target size.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I compress a photo to 20 KB for free?

    Yes. Choose Under 20 KB, press Compress Image and download the result. There is no signup, no watermark and no limit.

    Why is the result a JPG file?

    JPG is the format that can be made very small while still looking like a normal photo, and almost every online form accepts it. PNG files cannot be reduced to an exact size in the same way.

    What happens to a PNG with a transparent background?

    JPG does not support transparency, so transparent areas become white. This is usually what forms want for photos and signatures.

    Will my photo lose quality?

    A smaller file always has less detail, but the tool keeps the highest quality that fits under your size. Check the preview, and if it looks too soft, pick a bigger target size if your form allows it.

    Does it work with iPhone photos?

    Yes, when you choose the photo from the Photos library in Safari, which gives the browser a JPG copy. If a HEIC file does not open in your browser, change the iPhone camera setting to Most Compatible or take a screenshot of the photo and compress that.

    Is my image uploaded?

    No. The image is compressed inside your browser on your own device, so it never leaves your phone or computer.
